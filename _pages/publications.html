--- layout: archive title: "Publications" permalink: /publications/ author_profile: true --- ## Dissertation --- ### [Disenfranchised Discontent: Exploring the Scope and Public Opinion of Criminal Voting Restrictions](https://irbe.library.vanderbilt.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/887302ef-2aa3-4bb3-995f-5d6131b6f130/content) *Vanderbilt University*, August 2021 Committee: Jonathan T. Hiskey, Ph.D. (Chair) · Elizabeth Zechmeister, Ph.D. · Allison Anoll, Ph.D. · Shane P. Singh, Ph.D. This dissertation examines the scope and consequences of criminal disenfranchisement across democratic political systems. The first empirical chapter constructs conservative country-level estimates of the global criminally disenfranchised population and demonstrates that the United States is a clear outlier both in the severity of its criminal voting laws and the size of its affected custodial population. It then exploits subnational variation in U.S. state law to show that higher rates of criminal disenfranchisement are negatively associated with democratic performance — states that disenfranchise larger shares of their residents tend to do a worse job of educating, enriching, and employing their citizens. The second empirical chapter uses national survey data to examine how felony convictions shape political attitudes and behaviors, finding that any positive effect of conviction on engagement is dampened for people living in states with post-prison voting bans. The third chapter investigates public support for criminal disenfranchisement reform, finding that — even among likely voters in two of the most restrictive southern states — majority opinion consistently favors less punitive laws than those currently on the books.
